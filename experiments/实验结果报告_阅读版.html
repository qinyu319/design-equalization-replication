2. 必须先处理：原 hub 选择器存在节点编号错误

原代码先取 g.degree() 的度数数组，再把排序后的位置当成节点编号。SBM 经过节点重标记后，节点遍历顺序不等于智能体编号，因此原程序可能把非 hub 节点作为 hub 接种。

在重放检查的两个 SBM 样本中，每次选择50人，旧算法分别有47人和46人的度数低于“最高50个节点”的度数门槛。对应的平均度数分别为5.64与6.14；按真实节点编号修正后为11.58与11.64。新实现按“度数降序、同度数时节点编号升序”选择；其他网络也可能因并列度数的规则变化而产生输出差异，因此已重算全部网络的 hub 单元。

8个原始存档样本的旧实现重放全部通过。纠错后的40,320次运行使用原有配置和原始种子，另外80,640行非 hub 运行完全保持不变；直接投放事件数也全部不变。

3. E1：剂量与时序独立交叉，补足最重要的识别证据

交叉因素为4种网络、剂量0.05/0.50/0.95、3种时序、强度0.06/0.14/0.18；分别在回归系数 μ=0 和0.05下分析。此处剂量指“人均累计直接投放事件数”，不是独立触达人数。强度是数值假设，不能解释成实测的AI质量水平。

4. E2：把主题落到可操作的治理假设

固定强度0.14，交叉两档剂量、两种时序及四种网络。比较标签衰减 λ=0.3/0.7、二次传播时标签保留/丢失，随机/hub接种、覆盖预算5%/20%、依从性50%/100%，以及两种组合干预。接种后的易感性乘以0.2。

在剂量0.05、连续投放、5%覆盖和100%依从时，hub优势只有约0.00000488，区间[−0.0000316, 0.0000413]跨零，不能概括成“hub在所有场景稳定优于随机”。Fig3 汇报全部预算、依从性和标签对照，而不仅是上述例子。分网络及其他结局也已保存。

这组实验最适合贡献为“治理评估的假设压力测试”：标签是否随二次传播保留、覆盖率与依从性怎样设定，会改变模型预测。衰减标签、降低易感性本来就是模型设定，因此不能把方向符合设定解释成发现了标签或接种的真实因果有效性。 绝对观点变化下降也不天然等于危害下降；若采用错误信息情境，应明确目标内容在该假设场景中被定义为有害。

不同治理措施缺少统一资源成本，因此不能把标签、接种及组合干预排成“成本效益榜”。本次没有外部平台数据、人工标注、LLM生成质量评测或真实用户实验。

5. E3：第二类动力学提供边界检验，不能声称经验外部验证

另行实现持续同伴平均与初始意见锚定的模型，灵感来自 Friedkin–Johnsen 的社会影响框架；本文的外部信息耦合是本次明确写出的扩展，并非复现某篇论文的全部模型。原始来源：Friedkin & Johnsen, 1990。精确更新式与对照定义见 MODEL_SPECIFICATION.md。

关键设计是每个有输入运行都减去同一网络、初始状态、锚定强度的无外部输入轨迹，避免把自然的同伴收敛误认为内容造成的变化。240个无输入对照在相对结局上严格为零。

锚定系数 a=0.2 和0.8 时，完整交叉设计中的时序分配分别为60.55%和60.42%；各自固定剂量的设计中约为81.81%–82.11%。这与原模型 μ=0 的弱时序分配形成对照。

以 a=0.2、剂量0.50为例，连续投放相对单次的终点变化增加0.004759，95%蒙特卡洛区间[0.004698, 0.004819]；但时间平均变化减少0.000434，区间[−0.000463, −0.000404]。早期投放有更长累计作用时间，持续投放在末期仍有输入，两个结局回答不同问题。

建议作为“Generality and boundary conditions”小节，强调框架在不同机制下允许得出不同结论。不能写成第二模型“验证了时序不重要”，也不宜称为真实世界 external validation。

6. E4：估计器可以校正点估计，但推断仍有边界

已知真值为 μ=0.8A+0.5B+0.6AB+0.3C，对应 Shapley 分配(0.82, 0.43, 0.09, 0)。四种噪声条件包含异方差、共同冲击及与因素方向相关的结构化冲击；重复数为10/30/60。每一设置1,000个数据集评估偏差，前500个评估区间覆盖，每个数据集399次不同原始重复对的重采样。

在结构化相关噪声、R=30、真实贡献为零的D因素上，in-sample平均分配为0.014838，complete-U为0.000313；complete-U均值的蒙特卡洛标准误为0.000475。该设置中65.3%的complete-U估计为负，因此近零时应保留负估计，不能截零后再解释成无偏。

百分位区间对非零因素的经验覆盖率范围为84.0%–95.2%，对零因素为98.0%–100.0%；jackknife-normal也没有统一可靠的校准。500次覆盖评估本身存在抽样误差：结果表附带二项式蒙特卡洛标准误与Wilson区间。Fig4展示零因素点估计，Fig5完整展示各因素覆盖率，不能只摘取有利设置。

需要修正的方法学叙述

对对称二次型矩阵 H、独立同分布重复向量 y_r、样本均值 ȳ 及按R−1分母估计的协方差 S，有严格恒等式：

U = Σ(r≠s) y_rᵀ H y_s / [R(R−1)] = ȳᵀ H ȳ − tr(H S)/R。

因此，若附录J的“noise subtraction”使用完整、未正则化的样本协方差，它与complete-U是同一个有限样本估计量，不能当成相互独立的竞争方法或声称后者在该比较中独具无偏性。若只减对角噪声或采用正则化，则需要另写定义与适用条件。本次用独立的正交对比实现以及投影矩阵实现交叉验证了点估计和重采样公式。

Suggested framing for the introduction

Simulation-based content governance depends on choices about exposure intensity, delivery schedules, intervention coverage, and behavioral response. When these choices are bundled into categorical conditions, mechanism attribution may reflect both the named mechanism and the quantitative inputs assigned to it. We study this problem as an audit of governance evaluation: the objective is to distinguish contributions induced by the experimental design from contributions that persist under controlled exposure and alternative dynamics. The simulations do not estimate the persuasive capabilities of particular language models or establish the real-world effectiveness of disclosure or inoculation. Their contribution is a reproducible procedure for exposing the assumptions under which governance comparisons change.

Methods: supplementary experiment design

We extended the original study with four computational experiments. All agent-based and peer-averaging experiments used 1,000 agents,50 time steps, and 30 independent replication blocks. The first experiment independently crossed topology (BA, ER, WS, SBM), cumulative direct dose (0.05,0.50,0.95 events per agent), schedule (single-shot, five-step burst, continuous), and message strength (0.06,0.14,0.18), separately for opinion-reversion coefficients 0 and 0.05. This yielded 6,480 runs. Direct dose denotes exposure events rather than unique recipients; repeated direct exposures and endogenous second-hand exposure were retained as distinct quantities. Message strengths were treated as synthetic numerical inputs, without empirical AI-quality labels.

The second experiment examined governance assumptions at strength 0.14, doses 0.05 and 0.50, and single-shot and continuous schedules across the four topologies. Fifteen primary configurations comprised no intervention; disclosure attenuation 0.30 or 0.70 with retained or lost second-hand labels; random or degree-targeted inoculation with target budgets 0.05 or 0.20 and compliance 0.50 or 1.00; and two combined policies pairing retained-label disclosure at 0.70 with either inoculation strategy at budget 0.05 and full compliance. Inoculation multiplied compliant agents' susceptibility by 0.20. The primary matrix comprised 7,200 runs;2,400 additional runs assessed the five hub-based policies using the archived selector. Comparisons of random and hub targeting matched target budgets and compliance. Disclosure and inoculation were not compared as equal-cost policies because their resource requirements were not represented in common units.

The third experiment used independently implemented peer-averaging dynamics inspired by the anchored social-influence framework of Friedkin and Johnsen (1990). With row-normalized adjacency W, initial opinion x_i(0), exposure indicator e_i(t), stubbornness a, and external coupling β, the update was

x_i(t+1)=a x_i(0)+(1−a)[(1−βe_i(t))Σ_jW_ijx_j(t)+βe_i(t)×0.6].

We crossed a∈{0.20,0.80}, β∈{0.10,0.30}, the three direct doses, three schedules, and four topologies. The 4,320 exposed runs were paired with 240 no-input controls sharing the graph, initial opinions, and stubbornness. Outcomes were measured relative to the paired no-input trajectory, preventing spontaneous peer convergence from being attributed to the external message. This experiment tests a boundary across model families; it is not empirical external validation.

We retained the original terminal outcomes and added the time average of population mean absolute displacement over the 50 post-update states. The original signed displacement is target-oriented: each agent's change is multiplied by sign(0.6−x_i(0)). Conversion is conditional on x_i(0)<0. For the peer-averaging extension, absolute and signed changes use the no-input endpoint as the reference, while conversion is the difference between exposed and no-input conversion rates among initially negative agents.

New random streams were deterministically derived from suite, topology, replication, and stream identifiers. Matched scenarios shared seed keys within a replication block; downstream random draws could diverge when interventions changed branching. Independent replication blocks, rather than individual agents or scenario cells, were the uncertainty unit. Paired differences were averaged over the remaining equally weighted design cells within each block and summarized using unadjusted 95% t intervals for the Monte Carlo mean. These intervals describe simulator precision, not empirical population uncertainty. Factor allocations used the complete-U estimator and 1,000 distinct-original-pair block resamples; the resulting percentile ranges are reported as sensitivity summaries rather than calibrated confidence intervals.

Methods: implementation audit and correction

Before interpreting the extensions, we replayed eight archived runs spanning all four topologies and two schedules. All checked scalar and trajectory outputs matched. We then identified an indexing defect in hub selection: positions in the graph-degree iteration were interpreted as agent IDs after SBM nodes had been relabelled. The corrected implementation ranks degree by explicit node ID, resolving ties by ascending ID, and makes zero budget or compliance a true no-op. As the tie rule can affect other topologies, all 40,320 archived hub runs were repeated with their original configurations and seeds. The other 80,640 rows were retained unchanged. The resulting 120,960-row corrected archive is versioned separately from the original and is not treated as additional independent evidence.

Results: separating direct dose from schedule

In the fully crossed design without reversion, dose received 70.57% of the complete-U allocation for terminal mean absolute displacement, compared with 0.21% for schedule,29.22% for strength, and 0.01% for topology(Figure 1). In contrast, with reversion 0.05, schedule received 44.14%, dose 39.40%, strength 16.46%, and topology 0.01%. These percentages are normalized within each new design and are not the fixed-benchmark quantities reported for the original archive.

At fixed dose 0.50, continuous minus single-shot terminal displacement was−0.001794(Monte Carlo 95% interval:−0.001980 to−0.001607) without reversion, but+0.009608(0.009536 to 0.009680) with reversion. Thus a small schedule allocation in the memoryless benchmark does not imply a universal absence of timing effects. Temporal response and the observation window determine whether early or ongoing exposure produces the larger terminal change(Figure 2).

Derived forward and reverse dose–schedule bundles from the same no-reversion response grid allocated 70.64% and 71.31%, respectively, to the schedule label. These are alternative design views rather than additional simulations. Their allocations demonstrate how a composite label can carry dose variation; they do not constitute a causal mediation decomposition or share an identical estimand with the full factorial game.

Results: governance comparisons conditional on exposure and implementation

At dose 0.50 under continuous seeding, hub targeting with a5% budget and full compliance reduced terminal absolute displacement by an additional 0.000504 relative to random targeting(95% Monte Carlo interval:0.000317 to 0.000691). At a20% budget, this difference was 0.000988(0.000762 to 0.001215). At dose 0.05, however, the corresponding 5%-budget difference was approximately 0.000005, with an interval spanning zero. The results therefore do not establish uniform hub superiority across exposure settings(Figure 3).

In the same high-dose continuous condition, retaining labels during second-hand exposure produced an additional displacement reduction of 0.001429(0.001382 to 0.001476) at attenuation 0.30 and 0.002906(0.002799 to 0.003012) at attenuation 0.70. These differences quantify sensitivity to the assumed persistence of disclosure. Because disclosure and inoculation attenuate specified model components by construction, they cannot independently validate real-world policy effectiveness. Full policy-by-topology and alternative-outcome results are provided in the computational supplement.

Results: model-family and outcome boundaries

In the peer-averaging extension, schedule accounted for 60.55% and 60.42% of the full-design terminal allocation at stubbornness 0.20 and 0.80, respectively. Within fixed-dose games, the corresponding schedule shares ranged from 81.81% to 82.11%. Timing therefore remained consequential after direct-dose equalization in these dynamics.

At stubbornness 0.20 and dose 0.50, continuous seeding increased terminal displacement relative to single-shot seeding by 0.004759(95% Monte Carlo interval:0.004698 to 0.004819), while decreasing time-averaged displacement by 0.000434(interval for the signed difference:−0.000463 to−0.000404). Terminal and cumulative outcomes answer different governance questions: an early perturbation can contribute more to the integrated trajectory while leaving a smaller residual at the terminal observation. The framework should detect such differences, rather than impose a common ranking across dynamics or outcomes.

Estimator validation under correlated noise

We evaluated the estimators on a balanced 2^4 design with cell mean 0.8A+0.5B+0.6AB+0.3C and true factor allocations(0.82,0.43,0.09,0). Four normal-noise covariance settings represented independent homoskedastic noise, independent heteroskedastic noise, heteroskedastic common shocks, and heteroskedastic structured shocks. Each setting used replication counts 10,30,60 and 1,000 datasets, yielding 12,000 datasets. For the first 500 datasets per setting,399 distinct-pair bootstrap resamples and delete-one-block jackknife-normal intervals were also evaluated. Dataset-level estimates, exact covariance matrices, bias Monte Carlo errors, interval widths, and coverage Wilson intervals are archived.

For the null factor under structured correlated noise at R=30, the mean in-sample allocation was 0.014838, compared with 0.000313 for complete-U; the Monte Carlo standard error of the latter mean was 0.000475(Figure 4). Complete-U estimates were negative in 65.3% of these datasets. Such negative estimates were retained rather than truncated.

Point correction did not ensure interval calibration. Across nonzero factors, empirical coverage of the nominal 95% distinct-pair percentile intervals ranged from 84.0% to 95.2%; for the null factor it ranged from 98.0% to 100.0%. Jackknife-normal coverage was also setting-dependent(Figure 5). Because each coverage estimate uses 500 datasets, its own Monte Carlo uncertainty must be considered. These findings support reporting the manuscript's allocation ranges as sensitivity summaries and do not establish a universally calibrated inferential procedure.

For any symmetric allocation matrix H and independent replication vectors, the complete-U estimator is exactly ȳᵀHȳ−tr(HS)/R, where S is the full sample covariance using denominator R−1. Accordingly, an unregularized full-covariance noise-subtraction estimator is algebraically identical to complete-U and should not be presented as an independent competing method. Diagonal-only or regularized corrections require separate definitions and assumptions.

Results: effect of the hub correction on the original analysis

In two inspected SBM samples,47 and 46 of the 50 nodes selected by the archived procedure had degrees below the true top-50 cutoff. After correction, the mean selected degrees rose from 5.64 to 11.58 and from 6.14 to 11.64. The corrected baseline retained a dominant schedule allocation for mean absolute displacement:87.1689%, compared with 87.1760% originally(Figure 6). The corrected CS-0 schedule allocation was 0.0241654% of corrected baseline total variation, and its total retained variation was 4.9109%. Thus the central benchmark pattern persisted, although all downstream numerical reporting should use the corrected archive consistently.

Discussion and limitations

The supplementary experiments refine the contribution from a universal mechanism ranking to a conditional audit of simulation-based governance evidence. Independent crossing separates dose from schedule, governance stress tests expose reliance on label persistence and intervention reach, and alternative dynamics reveal settings in which timing remains important. The primary recommendation is to specify the intervention, exposure distribution, dynamics, and outcome window before interpreting factor importance.

No observed platform diffusion, measured language-model performance, or participant responses were used to calibrate these experiments. Opinion displacement is not intrinsically a harm measure; a misinformation interpretation requires an explicitly hypothetical harmful-target scenario. The peer-averaging extension has a different response mechanism and scale from the original simulator. Monte Carlo intervals exclude uncertainty about the assumed behavioral parameters and do not adjust for multiple comparisons. The reported governance contrasts therefore support model auditing and hypothesis development, not empirical effectiveness or safety claims.
